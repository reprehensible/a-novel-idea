# Supporting Cast

> Minor named characters (one-scene) go straight to the glossary.

## Wystan Crake
Harrowmere's guild journeyman kenner; 60s; kind, limited, content — the
good life the Stillfaith promises. Taught Joss his letters. Sends the
guild-word about the surveyor's case (ch 5) that draws the wolves. Dies
shielding Joss in the night raid (ch 7): the first price, and the knot in
Joss's graving (ch 67). Voice: patient, proverb-prone, chalk dust on his
sleeves.

## Tovan Ninth-Cairn
Oru-Vael guide, 20s; met stranded in Vell (Part II). Left his cairn-line
under a **memory-debt** (he fled a Scald crossing that killed his uncle;
the Count records him absent) — going home is the hard part of his road
(ch 32). Knows the quiet roads; twice saves the company with chant-maps.
Survives; maimed at the gate-fight (ch 56, loses two fingers, keeps the
joke about them); pays his debt at the end by telling the whole tale into
the Long Count (ch 74). Voice: cheerful fatalism, road-proverbs, terrible
puns in trade-tongue.

## Caul of the Tarn
Fourfold-graven hermit (Part III) — the strongest living person the company
meets, and a warning about depth: graved to be *unfindable* among other
things, he is met only because he permits it. Narrowed to near-inhumanity:
his constraints forbid him nearly everything (he cannot be found, cannot
leave the tarn's bowl, cannot touch iron — map his gravings' web before
drafting ch 37). Confirms what the Latch is; takes a price (ch 38): **a
promise from Joss, unnamed on page** — sequel Chekhov, log exact wording
when drafted. When the Dawn strikes (ch 39), he shields the company in
violation of his own graven constraints and unravels on page: the book's
one full demonstration of what contradiction costs. Voice: long silences;
sentences assembled from disused parts.

## Adlen Marsh
The surveyor. Dawn field-archivist who balked when he understood what the
Latch and the Bell meant; stole the Latch and ran west. Dies of
overspending (frost-wounds — he pressed past his depth to stay ahead)
in Wystan's back room (ch 5). His **journal** travels with the case:
partial truths in a dead man's voice, the story's recurring epistolary
seed (ch 16, 19, 43). Wrote one clear sentence the book returns to: *"They
are not wrong about the wonder. They are wrong about the price."*

## The Fenn family
- **Bram** (father): wounded in the raid (ch 7), recovers lame; proud,
  wordless, sends Joss off with the good knife.
- **Lise** (mother): the family's spine; her letters (sparse, practical,
  devastating) arrive ch 29, 44, 74.
- **Davin** (elder brother): conscripted ch 1; serves on the Seam; alive
  at book's end — his one letter (ch 44) shows the ordinary war the plot
  bypasses. Sequel-relevant witness to the armies feeling the
  Silence-echo.
- **Petra** (sister, 14): sharp, letter-quick — quietly the family's best
  kenning prospect; a sequel thread the coda touches (ch 76).
